In this lab, the easiest part was doing the more basic sed commands like deleting lines and replacing words. 
Once I understood the general format of sed, those commands were pretty straightforward. The hardest part 
was using regular expressions for more complicated patterns, like trying to make sure I matched the correct 
text without affecting anything else in the file. Task 3 was probably the most difficult because I had to 
think carefully about how to capture different parts of the phone number and then put them back in the correct
format.The sed actions that seem most useful to me are substitution and deletion. I can see both of these 
being used often when cleaning up files. Some of the commands on the notes sheet could mimic the behavior of 
other commands. Sed could mimic cat by simply printing the whole file. It could mimic tr in some situations 
by replacing characters or patterns with substitution commands. It could mimic grep by printing only lines 
that match a pattern. In the future, I could use sed when working with log files, scripts, configuration 
files, or data that needs to be cleaned up quickly.
